The research

Can a small model be both accurate and honest?

Research question. Can a transparent machine-learning model classify angiographic heart-disease presence in a small historical dataset while making its influential features, uncertainty, and subgroup error patterns explicit?

Objective

Evaluate models transparently using global and local explanations, uncertainty communication, error analysis and exploratory subgroup auditing.

Problem

On small clinical datasets a single accuracy number can hide where a model fails and for whom.

Why transparency matters

People need to see which inputs drive an output, how unsure it is, and where errors cluster before trusting any model.

What CardioLens investigates

Three models compared against a baseline, with explanations, uncertainty bands and subgroup error rates, all computed by executed code.

Cross-validation and model selection

Five-fold stratified cross-validation ran on the training data only. Model comparison and selection use those folds. Hyperparameters were fixed in advance and the test set was never used to choose models, tune or select features.

Data leakage prevention

Imputation, scaling and one-hot encoding sit inside scikit-learn pipelines, so they are fit on training folds only. Unknown categories are ignored safely.

Models and class handling

Dummy (most frequent class), L2 Logistic Regression with scaling, Random Forest with 300 trees. Balanced class weights; no SMOTE since the classes are close to balanced.

Evaluation metrics

Accuracy, precision, recall, F1, macro F1, ROC-AUC, confusion matrix, false-positive and false-negative rates, and cross-validation mean and standard deviation. Threshold 0.5.

Limitations

What this work cannot tell you

  • Small dataset: 303 patients and a 61-patient test set; one split, no confidence intervals computed.
  • Historical data from a single clinic (Cleveland subset only), so limited generalisability and possible dataset bias.
  • Small subgroup sample sizes make comparisons unreliable.
  • No external or clinical validation.
  • Model probabilities were not checked for calibration.
  • Feature importance and SHAP describe model behaviour, not medical causation.

Ethical considerations

No real patient data is collected or stored. Outputs describe model behaviour on public historical data, not risk to a person. Subgroup error rates are shown for auditing and are not evidence that any group is or is not treated fairly. The prototype is not intended for diagnosis, screening, treatment or healthcare decisions.
